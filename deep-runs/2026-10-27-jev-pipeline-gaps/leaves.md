## Audit: expert-missing items
- Transcription stage has no human reference transcripts per language, accent, device and game-audio level, so word error rate is unknown and a per-question accuracy figure cannot separate ASR error from extraction error.
- Rows that are low-confidence, 'Other', vendor-refused or parse-repaired go straight from extraction into the answers table; no sampled human spot-check (for example 2% of rows per game per week, 100% of a new game's first cohort) gates publication.
- Text-to-SQL planner sees the answers table with no data dictionary: question ids, option ids, the null codes 'not processed / not observed / not applicable' and the 'players who reached step N' denominator are undocumented, so each query re-derives 'tutorial churn rate'.
- Studio asks 'how many players churned in the tutorial?' and the chatbot silently picks one reading (quit the session, skipped the tutorial, never returned) without asking back or stating the definition in the answer.
- Post-session questionnaire ('would you keep playing?', self-rated confusion) and recruitment screener (genre experience, age band, platform) are collected by playtest ops but never joined to the session row, so extracted churn answers cannot be checked against self-report or segmented by experience.
- Game is hot-patched or a remote-config tutorial variant flips at video 4 of an 8-video session, but build_id is tagged once per session, so compare-two-versions credits post-patch behaviour to the old build.
- Speech-to-text keeps only words, so raised volume, rising pitch, faster speech and exhales over neutral words ('okay. okay. OKAY.') never reach the 'expressed frustration' question; no per-utterance acoustic feature column exists.
- When on-screen frames are added to fix the visual loss, no rule fixes frame sampling rate against sub-second tutorial cues, OCR language for localised UI, alignment of frame time to utterance time across the 15:00 cut, or an answer_source column (audio, vision, both), so audio-only and vision-derived rows are summed in one cohort count.
