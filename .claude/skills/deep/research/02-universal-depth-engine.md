# Research 2: a topic-agnostic "depth engine" — getting everything out of the model on any topic

Date: 2026-10-26
Builds on: scenario-council-research.md (same folder). Cost is not a constraint.

---

## 1. The reframed problem

Requirement change: the topic is open-ended (PRD gaps today, UX review tomorrow, churn, a video, anything). So the system cannot carry hand-written checklists per domain. It has to **generate its own decomposition** for whatever topic it is given, and then **refuse to stop at the summary level**.

Three model defaults are the enemy, and all three are topic-independent:

| Default | What it looks like | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Typicality** | The model gives the most typical answer, not the full distribution. | Verbalized Sampling (ICML 2026, arXiv 2510.01171) traces "mode collapse" to **typicality bias in preference data**: human raters prefer familiar text, so aligned models learn to produce it. |
| **Altitude matching** | A high-level question gets a high-level answer. "What am I missing in this PRD?" yields "consider error handling, accessibility, analytics". | STORM (NAACL 2024): directly prompted models "produce basic What / When / Where questions that only address surface-level facts"; giving the model **perspectives** produces deeper questions. |
| **One pass** | No step asks "what is still missing?" and goes back. | Deep research agents (LangChain Open Deep Research, DeepSearcher, survey arXiv 2506.18096) all converge on a plan, search, **reflect on gaps**, loop design. Question decomposition + iterative planning are "crucial". |

A generic engine therefore needs four invariants, in order. Each one is a counter to one default. None of them depends on the topic.

1. **Decompose before you generate**, and generate the decomposition itself.
2. **Generate from the tail of the distribution, under quotas**, not from the mode.
3. **Expand every item until it passes a leaf test**; the leaf test is the only thing written per run.
4. **Audit for gaps and loop** until yield drops.

Then contest, score, select (that part is unchanged from document 1).

## 2. The four invariants, with the technique behind each

### Invariant 1: generated decomposition (perspectives × axes)

Two different decompositions, both generated by the model for the topic at hand:

**Perspectives (who would notice what).** This is STORM's move: before researching a topic it "discovers diverse perspectives" by looking at similar topics, then personifies the LLM with each perspective and lets it ask questions in a simulated conversation with an answerer. Reported effect versus an outline-only baseline: +25 points on "organised", +10 points on "broad coverage". In our setting a perspective is "a kind of person who would notice something others would not": for a PRD that might be the support engineer who reads tickets, the finance person who models the cost, the user who has 10,000 rows, the lawyer, the person doing the data migration.

**Axes (how the topic varies).** This is **morphological analysis** (Zwicky): choose the parameters of the problem, list the values of each, and the cross product is the scenario space. Even 5 parameters with 4 values each give 1,024 cells; a consistency pass prunes the impossible ones. For a table feature: {item count} × {per-item status} × {aggregate status} × {user action} × {network state}. For churn: {lifecycle stage} × {segment} × {trigger event} × {observable signal}.

Perspectives give **diversity** (who is looking). Axes give **recall** (every cell gets looked at). The two together replace hand-written checklists: the grid *is* the checklist, and it is regenerated per topic.

**Then critique the decomposition itself** before using it. One agent only sees the perspective list and axis grid and answers: "which kind of person is missing, which parameter is missing, which value range is missing". Cheap, and it is where the "white thumbnail" class of miss gets prevented: a "visual / perceptual" perspective and an "image content" axis get added before any scenario is generated.

### Invariant 2: tail sampling under quotas

**Verbalized Sampling** (Zhang et al., Stanford, ICML 2026): instead of asking for *an* answer, ask for a **set of answers each with its own probability**, and instruct the model to sample from the tail ("each response with probability less than 0.10"). Reported 2 to 3x diversity gain with no measured loss of quality; "more capable models benefit more". The repo's exact instruction:

> Generate 5 responses to the user query, each within a separate `<response>` tag. Each `<response>` must include a `<text>` and a numeric `<probability>`. Please sample at random from the tails of the distribution, such that the probability of each response is less than 0.10.

For us the "response" is a finding, and k is large (20 to 40), so the instruction becomes "list 30 things a [perspective] would notice about [topic] in cell [axis values]; give each a probability that a typical reviewer would mention it; at least two-thirds must be below 0.10". That single sentence is the most direct counter to typicality the literature offers, and it costs nothing.

**Quotas** do the rest. "At least N" forces the model past the first five obvious items. Diversity research (document 1) shows independent parallel agents each producing a long list beat one agent refining. Keep that.

### Invariant 3: the leaf test and the altitude ladder

This is the invariant that stops summarisation. Every item is classified on an altitude ladder:

| Level | Name | Example (PRD gap) | Example (churn) |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | Topic | "the onboarding PRD" | "churn" |
| L1 | Category | "error handling is thin" | "pricing drives churn" |
| L2 | Mechanism | "partial failures in bulk operations aren't specified" | "users on the annual plan churn when their usage drops before renewal" |
| L3 | **Specific instance** | "in the Runs table, when 2 of 5 submitted runs fail, the PRD does not say what the aggregate row shows, whether retry is per-row, or whether the batch is 'done'" | "annual-plan users whose weekly active sessions fall below 1 in the 30 days before renewal: what fraction renew, versus those above 3?" |
| L4 | **Observable / test** | "Acceptance: aggregate row shows 'partial (3/5)'; retry button appears on failed rows only; batch status is 'partial' not 'done'" | "SQL on sessions × subscriptions; threshold hypothesis: renewal < 40% for that cohort" |

**Leaf test: an item is a leaf only when it is at L3 or deeper, names at least one concrete object from the supplied artifacts (a screen, a field, a sentence in the PRD, a timestamp in the video, a metric), and someone could act on it tomorrow without asking a clarifying question.**

Anything above L3 is not output. It is a node that must be **expanded into children**. This is recursive decomposition with an explicit stop rule, which is what the planning literature does: ReCAP stops recursing "once a subtask maps to an executable action"; ReLTEx (taxonomy expansion) stops a branch "when fewer than a minimum number of children pass a confidence check"; Micro-Act adds a hard depth and turn budget. Our stop rules, in order:

1. Leaf test passes (stop, emit).
2. Depth 4 reached (emit the best child, mark "shallow").
3. Saturation: a node's children add no new concrete object that a sibling already named (stop, prune).

The leaf test is the **one thing written per run**, by the framing agent, in one or two sentences, because "concrete object" and "act on it tomorrow" mean different things for a PRD, a screenshot set, a video and a churn dataset. Everything else in the engine is fixed.

### Invariant 4: gap audit loop

After generation and expansion, an **auditor** that saw none of the generation reads only the grid and the leaf list and answers three questions:

- Which grid cells have zero or one leaf? (mechanical)
- Which perspective produced leaves that are all in the same two cells? (that perspective was under-used)
- "An expert in [topic] reads this list. What is the first thing they say is missing?" (judgement)

Empty cells and the expert's answer go back to generation as targeted briefs ("cell: {N=many} × {some fail} × {user cancels mid-way}; produce 10 leaves"). Loop at most 3 times, or stop early when a loop yields under 10% new leaves after dedupe. That is DeepSearcher's "informational gaps → repeat or stop" decision, with a measurable stop.

**Chain-of-Verification** belongs here too, but for a different purpose: CoVe fixes *errors in what was said*, not omissions. So it runs after the audit, per leaf: "what in the artifact supports this; quote it", and leaves without support get a "speculative" tag rather than being deleted (speculative leaves are often the valuable ones, see "white thumbnail").

## 3. The engine, end to end

```
A  Frame          1 agent        topic + artifacts -> unit of output, leaf test, audience, evidence list
B  Perspectives   1 agent + VS   12 obvious perspectives + 8 tail perspectives (p < 0.10)
C  Axes           1 agent        5-8 parameters, 3-8 values each, consistency-pruned grid
D  Decomp critique 1 agent       "what perspective / parameter / value is missing" -> patch B and C
E  Generate       P agents, parallel, isolated   one per perspective; walks the grid; quota; VS; cites artifacts
F  Expand         per item       altitude-classify; expand < L3 until leaf test / depth 4 / saturation
G  Merge          1 cheap agent  dedupe, cluster, tag with cells + perspectives
H  Audit          1 agent        empty cells, under-used perspectives, expert's "what's missing" -> loop E for gaps (≤3)
I  Verify         per leaf       CoVe-style: quote the supporting artifact or tag "speculative"
J  Contest        proposer / skeptic / judge, 2 rounds, top-K only
K  Score          judge x3, median; rubric chosen in A (FMEA for risks; impact × confidence × cost for opportunities)
L  Select         human sees a table of leaves, decides build / defer / accept / reject
```

Stages A to D are the part that makes it topic-agnostic. Stages E to I are the part that makes it deep. J to L are from document 1.

### The non-negotiable output contract (the anti-summary rules)

Put these in every generation and expansion agent's prompt. They are blunt because the failure they prevent is blunt.

- Output is a list of leaves in the schema. No prose, no introduction, no conclusion, no "overall".
- Every leaf names at least one concrete object from the artifacts, quoted or pointed to (file, screen, PRD section, timestamp, column).
- Banned words unless followed by a specific instance in the same line: *improve, ensure, consider, robust, handle, proper, appropriate, various, etc., such as, and so on, best practices, edge cases*. If you wrote "etc.", expand it.
- No category heading with fewer than five leaves under it; if you can only find three, the category is the leaf's L1 tag, not a section.
- A number wherever the thing is measurable (counts, thresholds, sizes, durations).
- Minimum N leaves (set in A; 30 per perspective is a sane default when cost is irrelevant).
- Do not rank, filter or judge likelihood. That is a later stage. Include things you think are improbable; mark them with a low probability instead of leaving them out.

## 4. The same engine on your three examples

The engine does not change. Only Stage A's outputs change, and they are generated, not written by you. Shown here so you can check that the generated framing would be sensible.

### 4.1 "What am I missing in this PRD?" (scenario thinking)

- **Unit:** a gap, i.e. a situation the PRD does not specify.
- **Leaf test:** names a PRD section or a screen, states the situation precisely, and states what is unspecified (behaviour, copy, state, owner).
- **Perspectives the engine would generate:** support engineer with the ticket queue, on-call SRE, the user with 10,000 rows, the brand-new user with zero data, the finance person modelling cost per run, the QA lead, the data-migration engineer, the accessibility reviewer, the abuse-team analyst, the localisation vendor, the sales engineer demoing it, the person who has to deprecate it in two years. Tail perspectives (p < 0.10): the user on a 2G connection, the user who uploads a 10-hour video, the compliance auditor.
- **Axes:** surface × actor × item count × status × network state × time (first use / steady state / after a year).
- **Where your two misses land:** "visual / perceptual" perspective × "media content" axis value "image is a single flat colour" gives the white play button. "Item count: many" × "status: some failed" × "action: retry" gives the 5-runs-2-fail table, plus its siblings (retry one, cancel mid-way, aggregate row).

### 4.2 "Review this UI for UX issues" (screenshots or a recording)

- **Unit:** a usability issue tied to one screen element.
- **Leaf test:** names the screen and the element (by position or label), the user action, what goes wrong, and which heuristic or principle it violates.
- **What the research says about this specific task:** multimodal models can do heuristic evaluation from screenshots (arXiv 2507.02306), but in a direct comparison GPT-4o found only **21% of the issues human experts found**, while adding 27 issues the experts had not (arXiv 2506.16345). Issue detection was moderately consistent (Kappa 0.50) but **severity ratings were close to random** (Krippendorff's alpha near zero, arXiv 2512.04262). Consequences: use the engine for recall (perspectives × a screen-by-screen grid will push far past that 21%), keep **severity for the human** in Stage L, and run detection three times and union the results rather than trusting one pass.
- **Perspectives:** first-time user, power user with keyboard, screen-reader user, colour-blind user, user on a 360px phone, user mid-interruption, the designer who made it (defends it), the support agent, Nielsen's ten heuristics as ten separate perspectives.
- **Axes:** screen × element × state (default, hover, focus, loading, error, empty, overflow) × device × input method.
- **Video:** the timeline is an axis. Sample frames at a fixed interval plus at every scene cut and every UI state change, and treat each sampled frame as a "screen" in the grid. A video gets "perspectives" like the viewer with sound off, the viewer at 2x speed, the viewer who joined at minute 4.

### 4.3 "Identify churn" (data-backed)

- **Unit:** a testable churn hypothesis.
- **Leaf test:** names a cohort definable in the data, a trigger or condition, a measurable outcome, and the query or analysis that would confirm it.
- **Perspectives:** the customer who just cancelled, the CSM who had the last call, the finance analyst, the growth PM, the engineer who shipped the last breaking change, the competitor's sales rep, the procurement person who did not renew the contract, the user who churned silently by stopping usage without cancelling.
- **Axes:** lifecycle stage × segment (plan, size, acquisition channel) × trigger type (price, bug, competitor, champion left, usage drop, failed payment) × observable signal × time-to-churn.
- **Tail sampling matters most here.** "Pricing" and "bugs" are the mode; the valuable hypotheses ("champion left the company", "the integration they relied on was deprecated by a third party", "they only ever used one feature and it moved behind a different menu") are in the tail.

## 5. Building it in Claude Code without per-domain files

Because the lenses are generated per run, there is no `.claude/agents/lens-*.md`. There is **one skill** (`/deep <topic>`), which holds the fixed engine prompts for stages A through L, and spawns the generation agents dynamically through the Agent tool with a prompt it assembles at runtime (perspective + grid + contract + leaf test). Each spawned agent gets its own context window, so the isolation that diversity needs is free.

Once the engine prompts have settled, move the orchestration into a **Workflow script**: `parallel()` over the generated perspective list for Stage E, `pipeline()` into F and G, and a loop for H that stops on the yield threshold. The Workflow tool enforces output schemas at each stage, which is exactly what the leaf schema needs.

Two Claude-specific choices worth making from the start:

- **Use the strongest model for A, B, C, D and H** (framing, decomposition, audit). Those are judgement stages and the VS paper's finding that "more capable models benefit more" applies. Generation agents in E can be the same model or cheaper ones; use at least two different models across the perspective set so the set does not share one typicality bias.
- **Separate the judge's model from the generators' model** where possible (judge self-preference bias, document 1).

## 6. How you know it is working

Measure the engine, not the vibes.

| Metric | How | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Leaf rate** | share of emitted items at L3 or deeper, checked by a classifier agent | > 90% |
| **Yield curve** | new leaves per audit loop after dedupe | loop 2 < 30% of loop 1, loop 3 < 10% |
| **Seed recall** | run on 20 past features / reviews where you know the misses; count how many misses appear as leaves and at what rank | the two known misses in the top 20 |
| **Novelty over baseline** | same topic as one ordinary chat prompt; count leaves that the baseline did not produce | majority of leaves are new |
| **Grounded share** | leaves with a quoted artifact vs "speculative" | report both; do not force to 100% |

Add every production miss to a `misses.md` with the perspective and cell that should have caught it, and feed that into Stage D's critique prompt as "known past blind spots". That is the learning loop; nothing is trained.

## 7. Honest limits

- On UX review, published recall of LLMs against human experts is low and severity judgement is unreliable. The engine raises recall; it does not make severity trustworthy. Keep a human on severity.
- Decomposition quality bounds everything downstream. If Stage B or C is weak, the grid is weak. That is why D (critique the decomposition) exists and why the strongest model goes there.
- "Everything" is unbounded; the stop rules (depth 4, saturation, yield threshold) are what make the run finish. Lowering them costs recall; raising them costs attention in Stage L. When cost is not a concern, bias toward recall and invest in Stages G to K so the human only reads a ranked table.

Build the `/deep` skill with stages A, B, C, D, E, F, G and H and the output contract, nothing else.
